# Research basis for the segmentation improvements

This review supports an experiment on this project's **CHAT utterance boundaries**, not a claim to reproduce published sentence-punctuation scores. The two downloaded corpora are OCSC and NYU–Emerson. The existing test partition and original ASR cohort have already been examined. They remain explicitly **reused benchmarks**. Reshuffling them cannot create a genuinely unseen final test.

## Existing weaknesses to address

The first model learned word embeddings from this relatively small dataset. Its inference used separate 256-word blocks, so a boundary near a block edge lost some context. Its class-weighted loss and coarse threshold search also require validation calibration; a high recall score alone can hide excessive segmentation. None of these observations licenses changes based on test labels.

The registered experiment therefore compares overlapping inference, a published pretrained sentence segmenter, a sparse lexical/rule/pretrained-score model, and a fine-tuned compact pretrained transformer with rule features. The exact plan, input hash, partitions, tuning limits and exclusions are in `outputs/validation/benchmark/improvement_protocol.json`. Any implementation deviation or failed candidate must be recorded in the results, rather than omitted to make the selected model look better.

## Task and metric safeguards

Inputs consist solely of the normalized words available at deployment, plus features computed from those words. Gold punctuation, casing, original utterance rows, adult-turn boundaries, timestamps and gold utterance lengths are not predictive inputs. Training windows are selected by token positions, not by gold utterance boundaries. Subtoken outputs must map back to the correct word gaps. The paragraph end is not counted as a detected internal boundary.

Selection uses validation boundary F1. Final reporting additionally includes exact utterance spans, false/missed boundaries, utterance-count error, per-child/corpus/task results and downstream MLUw/lexical errors. A 67% boundary F1 does not mean 67% of utterances have both boundaries correct. Bootstrap uncertainty must resample children rather than dependent word gaps.

Every segmenter must preserve the source words and character spans so the common TAALED scoring and CHAT/CLAN export can still operate. Same-input calculator validation is separate from segmentation accuracy. MLUm, timing-based WPM, PGU, omission/substitution and maze measures continue to require adequate corresponding annotations; sentence segmentation does not supply them.

Acoustic/prosodic models and real ASR adaptation are relevant directions, but this experiment has transcript inputs. Gold timing must not be used to imply audio-level performance. A new, unexamined corpus or independently collected cohort is needed for a confirmatory final claim.

## Reassessment from first principles

The prediction target is a **transcript annotation convention**: mark word gaps corresponding to the reference CHAT utterances. It is not generic topic segmentation, vocal-activity detection, forced phone alignment, or written-sentence detection. A word stream may admit several plausible boundaries without the missing prosody and conversational context. Gold CHAT labels are references for their own corpus, not proof of a universal SALT C-unit standard.

The new design retains every source word, represents each candidate gap with pretrained context from both sides, and includes the existing rules as a learned input feature. It compares independent class decisions with a learned linear-chain sequence decoder. Both choose the highest-scoring class/sequence; there is **no validation search for a probability cutoff**, hard maximum utterance length, or hand-selected pause duration. Training hyperparameters and crop lengths are ordinary declared experimental settings, not clinical segmentation thresholds.

Training includes the original paragraphs plus two seeded word-position crops per training paragraph. The crops include different context lengths and do not use gold boundaries to choose their location or length. The final gap is unscored and rules are recomputed from the cropped words. This aims to reduce dependence on one context length without inventing ASR or grammatical-error labels. A stronger, pinned MiniLM encoder is compared with compact BERT; a CRF candidate learns sequence transitions during training. A fixed equal parameter average of the final three epochs is also scored when available. This is a simple averaging candidate, not a reproduction of the cyclical-learning-rate SWA algorithm.

The protocol and every validation candidate are recorded in `outputs/validation/benchmark/reassessment.json`. Training, epoch selection, parameter averaging and final selection use only the same participant-disjoint TalkBank train/validation groups. The original 10-participant cohort is final evaluation only. Both that cohort and the public test benchmark were examined historically, so neither can retrospectively become pristine. The existing private-developed rules are an inherited feature, another reason not to claim completely independent validation. No further rules, thresholds, weights, ensembles, or deployment routing will be chosen from this round's private results.

The final evaluation is one continuous target-speaker paragraph per labeled recording (14 recordings, 10 participants), matching the user's accepted setup. Speaker-turn-constrained transfer is a separately declared secondary analysis. It must not be substituted for the paragraph result. Existing workbook defaults remain the established rules regardless of the new private results; the new paragraph model is selected solely from public validation. Missing morphology, timing, grammaticality, error and maze evidence remains missing. Metric definitions and TAALED preprocessing are preserved.

### What would justify saying our system is better?

A defensible claim compares frozen systems on the **same participants, input words, available speaker context, annotation units, exclusions and metric implementation**, with participant-level uncertainty. The local CHATUtterance checkpoint comparison addresses part of this requirement, but it does not rerun every component of Batchalign. No cross-paper result here establishes overall superiority. The appropriate current wording is that our method has measured performance on these data and that comparable automated LSA systems exist, with different limitations and evaluation settings.

### Human comparisons and metric-first benchmarks (verified October 15, 2026)

For downstream metric error, **IDEAS is the most useful published numerical comparison** above: Table 4 reports children's own-talk median absolute relative errors of 11.11% for utterance count, 9.29% for MLU, 29.17% for unique words, 30.00% for words and 17.43% for TTR. Its word-based MLU uses words/utterances; errors are assessed on six-minute audio segments with manual timestamps and Whisper transcription. Our paragraph experiment holds words fixed and averages recordings within child before taking the median. Present these as contextual benchmarks with both protocols stated; do not compute a purported head-to-head improvement percentage.

The closest automated CHAT segmentation precedent remains **Batchalign**: its Delaware MCI F1 is 86.9%, with a table/prose discrepancy of 85.0/85.1% for controls. The original paper's other-system rows concern other evaluations; they are not common-data competitors. Our local checkpoint adapter is separately labeled and does not rerun the full pipeline.

For a literal human **transcription WER** reference, [Xiong et al., *Achieving Human Parity in Conversational Speech Recognition*, revised February 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.05256v2) reports professional-transcriber WER **5.9% on Switchboard** and **11.3% on CallHome**, both adult NIST 2000 telephone-speech subsets. These are valid WER benchmarks for those recordings, not child/autism error norms, utterance-segmentation scores, or expected MLU errors. Audio work remains deferred in this project.

For a human-level claim, a future evaluation should have multiple blinded annotators segment the **same deployment input**, predeclare CHAT-unit/exclusion rules, and score system–human and human–human boundaries with the same alignment and gap masks. Independent reference adjudication and participant-level metric agreement would then address downstream effects. Published agreement percentages alone cannot establish human-level performance. Numerical records and sources are in `outputs/validation/benchmark/literature_benchmarks.json`. No private final labels were opened to choose models or objectives for this literature update.
