# Reference and claim audit

Audited on October 8, 2026 against the current conference manuscript. Reference numbers below match the paper.

All 15 references correspond to identifiable publications or official resources. I found no fabricated title, author list, DOI, journal, page range, or book edition in the bibliography. However, existence is different from support for a particular claim. Several passages needed narrower wording or additional qualifications, and those corrections have been applied. The literature audit checks the cited sources. A subsequent final pass also independently recomputed the reported arithmetic and bootstrap summaries and regenerated the primary-human comparisons; neither check establishes clinical validity.

The bibliography contains 13 journal articles, one ASHA guidance page, and one book/manual citation. It is not a list of 15 experiments evaluating the same pipeline. Autism sampling, child ASR, clinical practice, lexical scoring, and agreement statistics each provide a different part of the foundation.

## Verification and access

“Full text” means the relevant methods, results, and limitations were accessible and checked, rather than inferred from a title or citation. “Limited access” means verification was restricted to a publication record, abstract, indexed material, or official documentation. The latter does not mean a reference is fabricated; it limits what can honestly be said about its verification.

## Corrections applied

1. Distinguished CLAN and SALT software from the SUGAR protocol.
2. Identified ELSA's ICCs as test–retest statistics for frequencies per minute.
3. Distinguished 33 enrolled participants from the 22 paired parent–examiner samples and added the source's short-sample qualification.
4. Distinguished Fox's correlations from agreement or percentage errors.
5. Added IDEAS's low MLU/TTR correlations and six-minute error aggregation.
6. Removed the implication that the TAALED-related studies validate our exact normalization and content/function scoring policy. Those details remain descriptions of our implementation.
7. Corrected the suggestion that the instruction involving “although” establishes a departure from CHAT. [CHAT section 9.2](https://talkbank.org/0info/manuals/CHAT.html) itself includes that conjunction. The manuscript now explains that raters marked utterances in Excel rather than producing fully coded CHAT transcripts. Subjective judgment and lack of blinding remain valid limitations.

## What the literature does and does not establish

- **SLP practice:** clinical tutorials and the survey support language sampling and computer-assisted analysis. They do not support a claim that all SLPs use the same workflow, that TAALED is standard clinical software, or that these tools diagnose autism.
- **100 utterances:** the manuscript appropriately presents this as a historically used sample size whose sufficiency depends on the measure, task, and population. No audited source validates our focused samples as representative of whole conversations or sufficient for utterance-length SD in autistic youth.
- **TAALED:** genuine software with documented research use. Its official documentation describes lemma forms, part-of-speech disambiguation, and content/function subsets. Research use is different from clinical norms or validation of our custom post-processing.
- **Prior results:** correlation, test–retest ICC, WER, and median absolute relative error answer different questions. Differences in sampling and aggregation prevent interpreting the published values as a leaderboard against our pipeline.
- **Novelty:** these references support particular components of the study. They do not establish that our pipeline is the first of its kind or that the literature search is exhaustive.
- **Our results:** published sources cannot verify the paper's own F1, error rates, bootstrap intervals, or participant counts. Those depend on our data and analysis scripts. They were preserved during the literature corrections. The subsequent numerical/source pass described below reproduced them from the saved score pairs and original source files.

## Remaining items before submission

1. Identify the exact MOSS system, provider/model version, and source publication or documentation. The current paper names it without a supporting reference. The repository did not identify an unambiguous model source, so no citation was invented.
2. Add a suitable primary spaCy/software citation and, if desired, formal references for the exact TAALED/pylats packages used. The TAALED desktop documentation and the taaled 0.32 Python package are related resources but should not be presented as the same version.
3. The unverified direct Brown attribution has been removed. The paper retains the actual written instructions and the checked CHAT/CLAN clause principles. Restoring Brown-specific attribution would require the exact publication and verification of the relevant conventions.
4. Obtain full copies of [3], [6], [10], [12], and [15] for a final author check if stronger claims are added. The present narrow claims are supported by the accessible abstracts, records, or official documentation; they should not be expanded on the assumption that the complete articles were verified.
5. A broader search of closely related segmentation and ASR-assisted CHAT/CLAN systems would be needed to support an exhaustive novelty claim. This audit was limited to the references already cited and the official documentation needed to check them.

No reference was deleted or renumbered. First appearances remain [1] through [15] in order. Changes were confined to explanatory prose; the abstract, study results, tables, figures, author block, and bibliography were retained.

## Final numerical and source verification

The final pass independently recalculated APEs, participant medians, displayed score medians, paired bootstrap intervals, boundary F1, kappa, and WER summaries. It also regenerated predictions from all 14 raw sampled recordings, re-read the primary human labels from column E, and recomputed all 11 complete-sample measures. Transcript word edits were recomputed from all 37 raw/corrected source pairs, and human interrater comparisons were recalculated from the original worksheets. The source workbooks and recordings were not modified.

The paper's reported values were reproduced: utterance-count MdAPE 6.31%, MLUw MdAPE 6.40%, length-SD MdAPE 16.76%; boundary F1 85.30% for the baseline and 88.64% for the current method; human interrater agreement 96.34%, kappa .834, and F1 85.47%. All three bootstrap reduction intervals include zero. Exact total-word error remains zero for each primary-human participant sample; it is a fixed-text boundary comparison, not independent evidence of transcription accuracy.

The final wording also makes the operational adverb rule explicit: adverbs ending in “-ly” and “well” count as content words, while other adverbs count as function words. This is a heuristic, not a complete identification of manner adverbs. The direct Brown attribution was removed because its original source had not been verified.

The model/provider/version behind the name MOSS remains unresolved. Participant diagnosis, ages, recording conditions, rater identities and conduct, and funding statements are author-supplied study information; software checks cannot independently establish those facts. The limited-access references listed above retain their stated verification limits.
